## Project overview

District Underground is a third-person hack-and-slash game tuned for fast combat, fluid movement, and satisfying score-chasing. Players dash, jump, and grapple through each level, chaining together robot takedowns while racing the clock.

This was our university capstone project and a **Released Student Game**, not a commercial release. The team brought it through a full production cycle: prototype, alpha, beta, gold, and final polish; then released it to the public on itch.io. We also demoed the game at Armageddon in New Zealand, which was a great opportunity to see fresh players engage with what we’d built.

## Combat, enemies and encounters

My main Game Design focus was on combat, enemy AI, and Encounter Design. I worked on enemy behaviours, combat roles, encounter composition, and boss behaviour, while level design was shared across the team.

The aim was to create enemies that supported fast movement and delivered clear, readable pressure instead of breaking the game’s pace. That meant designing for how behaviours combined within an encounter, not just how each enemy functioned on its own.

## AI programming and tools

I handled programming for both enemy and gameplay systems - building out a Behaviour Tree framework, an Environment Query System-style tool inspired by Unreal's approach, a simple AI director influenced by Left 4 Dead and Doom, plus a set of debugging tools to support design iteration.

Working on tools and using them within the same project was an early lesson in technical design: interface clarity, solid debugging, and fast iteration all shape what designers can actually experiment with.
