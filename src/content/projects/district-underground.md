## Project overview

District Underground is a third-person hack-and-slash game built around fast combat, movement and score-chasing. Players dash, jump and grapple through levels, chaining robot kills while trying to finish quickly.

This was our university capstone and a **Released Student Game**, not a commercial title. The team took it through a full production cycle — prototype, alpha, beta, gold and polish — before releasing it publicly on itch.io. We also demonstrated the game at Armageddon in New Zealand, which provided a valuable chance to watch unfamiliar players engage with it.

## Combat, enemies and encounters

My main Game Design focus was combat, enemy AI and Encounter Design. I worked on enemy behaviours, combat roles, encounter composition and boss behaviour, while level-design work was shared across the team.

The goal was to create enemies that supported fast movement and readable pressure rather than interrupting the pace. That meant thinking about how individual behaviours combined in an encounter, not only how each enemy worked in isolation.

## AI programming and tools

I also programmed enemy and gameplay systems. This included a Behaviour Tree framework, an Environment Query System-style tool inspired by Unreal's approach, a simple AI director influenced by Left 4 Dead and Doom, and supporting debugging tools.

Building the tools and using them in the same project was an early lesson in technical design: the interface, debugging information and iteration speed all shape what designers can realistically explore.
